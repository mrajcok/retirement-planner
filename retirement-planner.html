Healthcare by year

Costs for both of you, per year, in today's dollars. Before 65 each person pays for ACA coverage; from 65, Parts B and D and Plan G. The HSA pays Medicare Part B and D premiums (and surcharges, if selected) from the age you choose.

How this is calculated

  • Everything is in today's dollars, so living expenses, Social Security, contributions, tax brackets, the standard deduction and Medicare surcharge thresholds all keep pace with inflation automatically.
  • Some amounts are fixed in law and don't rise with inflation: the $250,000 net investment income tax threshold and the Social Security taxation thresholds. These shrink each year in today's dollars at the general inflation rate. Brokerage cost basis also shrinks, since inflation creates taxable gains.
  • Each year's expenses are living costs plus healthcare plus Medicare surcharges. Eligible Medicare costs come from the HSA (from the chosen age); the rest comes from the brokerage account (up to the yearly limit), then Social Security, then pre-tax withdrawals. Taxes are paid from the pre-tax withdrawal.
  • Healthcare: coverage before 65 is per person per year. From 65, each person pays Parts B and D and Plan G. Dental applies to each person every year. Costs grow at the healthcare rate minus general inflation, starting today, and Medicare surcharges grow at the same rate. Part A is assumed premium-free.
  • The HSA is invested at the same return, grows tax-free, and is taxed to heirs like pre-tax money.
  • Conversions raise ordinary taxable income to the chosen target. Capital gains from brokerage sales are taxed on top at 0/15/20%, plus the 3.8% net investment income tax.
  • Social Security taxation uses the actual provisional-income formula. Spousal benefits start once both the spouse has reached her claiming age and you have filed.
  • Medicare surcharges (IRMAA) use income from two years earlier. Required minimum distributions use the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table.
  • Extra RMD money you don't spend is reinvested in the brokerage account. Heirs' value counts Roth and brokerage in full and pre-tax money after the heirs' tax rate.
  • Future tax increase adds the chosen percentage points to every ordinary income bracket from the chosen age, and to the heirs' rate. Capital gains rates are left unchanged.
  • Not modeled: state income tax, ACA premium subsidies before 65, itemized deductions, market ups and downs, and future tax law changes.
This is an educational model, not financial or tax advice. Confirm conversion amounts each year with a CPA or planner before acting.
